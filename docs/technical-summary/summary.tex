\vspace{-2pt}

\noindent\textbf{\textit{Abstract}}\,---\,Robots learn skills from human teleoperated demonstrations, which are slow and costly. \textbf{Scaffld removes the person:} a cloud teacher agent (Argon) turns one typed sentence into a checked task, demonstrates it by driving the robot, keeps only demos an independent physics validator accepts, and fine-tunes a small on-device student. On a Unitree G1 humanoid in MuJoCo, \textbf{one sentence taught the robot a new skill in 39.7 minutes} (wall-clock stage time): 56 accepted demos, a 21-minute fine-tune, and a 500M-parameter student that went from \textbf{0/20 to 18/20} on sealed, unseen scenes (\textbf{17/20} with the bin moved). Given a grey image instead of its camera, it scores \textbf{0/20} (on a Mac). It runs alone on a laptop GPU at \textbf{0.31\,s per decision, with no cloud calls}.

\section{User and problem}
\head{Users:} robotics teams that must teach a robot a new task, or fix one it fails. \head{Problem:} that takes costly human demonstrations. DROID needed 50 collectors for 12 months (76k trajectories)~[1]; RT-1 needed 13 robots for 17 months (130k episodes)~[2]. \head{Our answer:} a teacher agent (Argon) that finds the gaps in a robot's skills, creates and validates its own lessons, and leaves a student that runs on the robot alone.

\section{System architecture and key features}
\begin{itemize}
\item \head{Robot and task.} A Unitree G1 (29 DoF) hugs a 0.8\,kg box off a table into a bin, twisting at the waist (\textit{fixed feet}) or walking 1.0--1.4\,m on Unitree's RL policy (\textit{walking}).
\item \head{Camera in, skills out.} Agents see only a 320$\times$240 head camera, the instruction and the last skill's feedback; ground truth reaches only the validator. They act through 10 whitelisted skills, run by IK and joint PD control.
\item \head{Teaching (Fig.~1).} One typed sentence $\rightarrow$ Argon (gemini-4-argon) writes a task spec and success rule, checked in simulation $\rightarrow$ parallel Argon teachers demonstrate $\rightarrow$ the validator keeps what passes and names failures in words $\rightarrow$ accepted steps fine-tune the student $\rightarrow$ exam alone on 20 sealed seeds.
\item \head{Key features:} mid-run box or bin moves that reach agents only as new frames; 13 worded verdicts; exact replay of accepted demos (56/56); a live dashboard where every number carries its $n$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\begin{itemize}
\item \head{One sentence $\rightarrow$ new skill.} From ``Stay where you are and put the box in the bin beside you, even if someone moves the bin'', 8 parallel teachers made 60 demos in 16\,min; the validator kept 56. After a 21-min fine-tune, the student went \textbf{0/20 $\rightarrow$ 18/20} on sealed scenes and \textbf{0/20 $\rightarrow$ 17/20} with the bin moved after the lift (Fig.~2a; McNemar $p<0.001$, paired by seed). \textbf{39.7\,min from sentence to tested skill} (wall clock, GPU queue excluded); a re-run reproduced every seed.
\item \head{It acts on what it sees.} Given a grey image instead of its camera: \textbf{0/20 vs.\ 19/20} (same seeds, on a Mac). Blind, it twists once and places, so its decision to stop comes from the image.
\item \head{Demos teach that decision.} Trained on only 14 or 28 of the same demos, it twists to its 150\textdegree\ limit and never places (0/20 each; Fig.~2b).
\item \head{Lessons aimed at failures work.} Recovery and box-moved demos (52 $\rightarrow$ 117) raised success with the box moved from \textbf{7/20 to 15/20}, and on unseen bin placements from \textbf{14/40 to 21/40} (overall $p=0.19$; bin further ahead 4/20 $\rightarrow$ 14/20, $p=0.006$). In round 1, Argon named the cause of all 10 failed trials.
\item \head{Inside the edge budget.} \textbf{0.31\,s median}, 0.56\,s p95 ($n=326$); 1.08\,s over a 35-min exam ($n=237$); 2 of its 2,145 decisions passed 2\,s (the first after loading). Peak memory \textbf{2.01\,GiB of 3\,GB} (Fig.~2c).
\end{itemize}

\section{Limits}
\head{Simulation only;} sim-to-real is next. \head{One task family,} learned at the skill level. \head{Walking:} the memoryless student has not yet learned when to stop turning. \head{Bin moves} in our tests stay within reach of its learned routine; larger moves are next. \head{Teaching needs the cloud;} the deployed student does not.

\section{Critical failure mode and safety}
\begin{itemize}
\item \head{Failure mode: acting on a stale picture of the world.} If the box or bin moves mid-task, a robot replaying its plan grasps air or drops the box; near people, it swings a load into space that is no longer clear. \textbf{Our agents keep no plan:} every skill is chosen from the newest frame, and recovery lessons more than doubled success with the box moved (Fig.~2a).
\item \head{When unsure, look again.} An unparseable answer becomes \texttt{reobserve}; two in a row end the episode.
\item \head{Safe motion.} Only whitelisted, clamped skills run. A move past a limit, an unreachable target or a walk into the table is refused before anything moves. The validator rejects falls, joint-limit violations and penetration, so unsafe demos never train.
\item \head{Data and secrets.} Sealed held-out seeds, an 800-call Argon cap per run, keys never logged; 600+ tests pin these rules.
\end{itemize}
